

# EC2 Pricing Models Part I:
    EC2 pricing models help you control cost based on how long and how predictably you use instances.

    # Why Pricing Models Matter:
        - EC2 is charged based on usage.
        - The right pricing model can save a lot of money.
        - The wrong choice can cause unnecessary cost.


    # On-Demand Instances:
        "Pay only for what you use, with no long-term commitment."

        # Key Points:
            - Pay per second or per hour.
            - No contract or commitment.
            - Highest flexibility.

        # Analogy:
            - Like taking a taxi.
            - Pay for the ride only when you need it.

        # Best For:
            1. Dev/Test environments
            2. New projects
            3. Unpredictable workloads
            4. Workloads that cannot be interrupted


    # Reserved Instances:
        "Commit for a longer period and get a lower price."

        # Savings:
            - Up to around 72% compared with On-Demand.

        # Commitment:
            - 1 year or 3 years.

        # Analogy:
            - Like a gym membership.
            - Commit for longer to save money.

        # Payment Options:
            1. All Upfront
                - Pay everything at the start.
                - Usually gives the highest discount.

            2. Partial Upfront
                - Pay some amount at the start.
                - Pay the rest over time.

            3. No Upfront
                - No large payment at the start.
                - Pay over the reservation period.

        # Best For:
            - Stable workloads running regularly for a long time.


    # Standard vs. Convertible Reserved Instances:
        1. Standard Reserved Instance
            - Less flexible.
            - Usually gives a bigger discount.
            - Best when your instance needs will not change much.

        2. Convertible Reserved Instance
            - More flexible.
            - Can change instance family, type, or other settings.
            - Usually gives a smaller discount than Standard RI.

        # Best For:
            - Production workloads running continuously for a year or more.


    # Spot Instances:
        "Use spare AWS capacity at a very low price."

        # Savings:
            - Up to around 90% compared with On-Demand.

        # Main Risk:
            - AWS can interrupt the instance.
            - You may receive only a short warning before termination.

        # Analogy:
            - Like a cheap standby airline seat.
            - Low cost, but your seat can be taken back.

        # Best For:
            1. Batch jobs
            2. Big data processing
            3. Fault-tolerant workloads
            4. Workloads that can be restarted

        # Not Best For:
            1. Databases that must stay available
            2. Critical production systems
            3. Workloads that cannot be interrupted


    # Quick Comparison:
        1. On-Demand
            - Savings --> Baseline price
            - Flexibility --> High
            - Risk --> Very low
            - Best For --> Dev/Test, new projects, unpredictable workloads

        2. Reserved
            - Savings --> Up to around 72%
            - Flexibility --> Low
            - Risk --> Commitment risk
            - Best For --> Stable, long-running production workloads

        3. Spot
            - Savings --> Up to around 90%
            - Flexibility --> Medium
            - Risk --> Interruption
            - Best For --> Batch and fault-tolerant workloads


    # Simple Selection Rule:
        1. If usage is new or unpredictable
            --> Start with On-Demand.

        2. If usage becomes stable and predictable
            --> Move to Reserved Instances.

        3. If the workload can be interrupted
            --> Use Spot Instances to reduce cost.


    # Lecture Summary:
        "Start with On-Demand for flexibility, use Reserved Instances for predictable long-term workloads,
        and use Spot Instances for low-cost workloads that can handle interruptions."
