# EC2 Pricing Models Part II:
    This lecture focuses on Savings Plans and practical ways to reduce EC2 cost.


    # Savings Plans:
        "Commit to a fixed hourly spend for 1 or 3 years."

        # Key Points:
            - Can provide large discounts.
            - More flexible than traditional Reserved Instances.
            - Savings apply based on your committed hourly spend.

        # Commitment:
            - 1 year or 3 years.

        # Discount:
            - Up to around 72% depending on the plan.


    # Savings Plans vs. Reserved Instances:
        Reserved Instances:
            - Usually linked more closely to specific instance choices.
            - Good for stable and predictable workloads.

        Savings Plans:
            - Based on committed hourly spend.
            - More flexible across eligible compute usage.
            - Better when workloads may change over time.


    # Two Types of Savings Plans:
        1. Compute Savings Plans
            - Most flexible option.
            - Can apply to:
                - EC2
                - Fargate
                - Lambda
            - Can work across different regions, instance families, and sizes.
            - Discount --> Up to around 66%.

        2. EC2 Instance Savings Plans
            - Less flexible than Compute Savings Plans.
            - Usually tied to:
                - One region
                - One instance family
            - Instance size and OS can still be flexible.
            - Discount --> Up to around 72%.


    # Combining Pricing Models:
        Real systems often use more than one pricing model.

        1. Web Servers Running 24/7
            --> Reserved Instances

        2. Interruptible Batch Jobs
            --> Spot Instances

        3. Dev/Test During Business Hours
            --> On-Demand
            --> Stop instances when not needed

        4. Workloads That May Change Over Time
            --> Compute Savings Plan


    # Cost Comparison Example:
        Example: m5.large in US East.

        1. On-Demand
            - Highest baseline cost.

        2. Reserved 1-Year
            - Lower cost.
            - Around 41% savings in the example.

        3. Reserved 3-Year
            - Even lower cost.
            - Around 63% savings in the example.

        4. Spot
            - Lowest cost in the example.
            - Around 69% savings.
            - Comes with interruption risk.

        Note:
            - Actual AWS prices can change.
            - Always check current pricing before making a decision.


    # Cost Optimization Strategies:
        # 1. Right-Size Before Committing:
            - Monitor usage first.
            - Choose the correct instance size.
            - Then commit to Reserved Instances or Savings Plans.

        # 2. Use AWS Cost Explorer:
            - Review usage history.
            - Use AWS recommendations.
            - Find opportunities to right-size or save money.

        # 3. Stop Unused Instances:
            - Dev/Test servers do not always need to run 24/7.
            - Stop them at night or when not in use.
            - This can reduce cost significantly.

        # 4. Use Auto Scaling + Spot:
            - Use Auto Scaling for changing demand.
            - Add Spot Instances for fault-tolerant workloads.
            - Helps reduce cost while still meeting capacity needs.

        # 5. Review Pricing Regularly:
            - AWS adds new services and pricing options.
            - Review your pricing strategy regularly.
            - Update your setup when better options become available.


    # Complete Pricing Model Reference:
        1. On-Demand
            - Discount --> Baseline
            - Commitment --> None
            - Interruptible --> No
            - Best For --> Dev/Test, unpredictable workloads

        2. Reserved
            - Discount --> Up to around 72%
            - Commitment --> 1 or 3 years
            - Interruptible --> No
            - Best For --> Stable production workloads

        3. Spot
            - Discount --> Up to around 90%
            - Commitment --> None
            - Interruptible --> Yes
            - Best For --> Batch, fault-tolerant workloads

        4. Savings Plans
            - Discount --> Up to around 72%
            - Commitment --> 1 or 3 years
            - Interruptible --> No
            - Best For --> Stable or evolving workloads


    # Simple Selection Rule:
        1. New or unpredictable workload
            --> On-Demand

        2. Stable long-term workload
            --> Reserved Instances

        3. Interruptible workload
            --> Spot Instances

        4. Long-term workload that may change instance type or service
            --> Savings Plans


    # Lecture Summary:
        "Savings Plans give long-term discounts with more flexibility than traditional reservations.
        The best AWS cost strategy is usually to combine On-Demand, Reserved, Spot, and Savings Plans
        based on workload behavior."
